What happened when you tapped

  1. Your phone switched on its NFC field at 13.56 MHz. A 4-turn coil etched into the back of the card picks it up, about 2.4 µH.

  2. The NFC chip, an NXP NT3H1101, answers your phone and turns part of that field into 2–3 V. That is the only power on the card. There is no battery.

  3. An STM32G030 microcontroller wakes up and pulls a 470 Ω dummy load in 100 kHz pulses, a little harder each step, until the voltage starts to sag. The highest power before that point is the reading.

  4. It lights the stars one LED at a time, each in its own 2 ms slot, so the peak current never exceeds what the field can deliver. The buffer on the supply rail is only 200 nF.

  5. Finally it writes the reading into the chip's memory, inside the web address you just opened.
